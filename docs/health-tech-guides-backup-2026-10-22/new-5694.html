How the NHS buys digital health: frameworks and routes

A rep’s guide to the national frameworks, lists and registries that sit between your software and an NHS contract, what stage each one is at, and the public notices that tell you a buyer is moving.

Start here

A framework award is not a sale. The call-off is.

Most NHS digital buying runs through frameworks. Getting onto one and winning work from one are two different events, with different notices and different people.

Framework award

The framework owner (for example the Government Commercial Agency, NHS Shared Business Services or NHS England) runs a competition and appoints suppliers to each lot. It is advertised with a tender notice and closes with a contract award notice.

  • Being appointed wins no money on its own. It gives you the right to be asked.
  • An open framework reopens on a published cycle, so a supplier that missed the first round can try again.

Call-off

A trust, Integrated Care Board (ICB) or general practice awards an actual contract under the framework. Find a Tender says that for an award under a framework no tender notice is published, and if there is a competition “only suppliers on the framework can submit proposals”.

  • Call-offs are by competition between the suppliers on a lot, or by an award without competition where the framework allows it.
  • The first public sign is usually the contract award notice, after the decision is made. By then it is too late to influence it.
What this means for a rep: the framework tells you who is allowed in the room. The selling happens before the call-off, with the clinical lead, the digital team and procurement at the trust or ICB, not at the framework owner. Frameworks set up under the old rules, before 24 February 2025, keep the old notice types, so you will still see “contract notice” and “contract award notice” labels on some of them.

Government Commercial Agency

Cross-government technology frameworks

The Government Commercial Agency (GCA) is the body that was Crown Commercial Service (CCS) until 1 April 2026. Its frameworks are open to health bodies as well as the rest of the public sector.

G-Cloud 15

Government Commercial Agency · RM1557.15 · open framework under the Procurement Act 2023
LIVE
Started
6 August 2026. It replaced G-Cloud 14.
End date
The agreement page lists 5 February 2028. The same page says it “will run for 4 years from 6 August 2026 (reopening after 18 months and again after 36 months)”.
Lots
1a infrastructure as a service (IaaS) and platform as a service (PaaS); 1b the same above OFFICIAL security; 2a infrastructure software as a service (I-SaaS); 2b other software as a service (SaaS), “applications hosted in the cloud”; 3 cloud support services.
How buyers call off
Award without competition, where a catalogue service meets a straightforward need and is chosen on lowest price; or a competitive selection process (two-stage or multi-stage) for complex or high-value needs.
Scale
GCA states over 4,000 suppliers and over 50,000 services.
What to do
  • Check your own listing. Ask your bid team which lot your product sits in and whether the service description matches what you sell today.
  • Know the reopening. If you are not on it, GCA says it reopens after 18 months. Plan the submission now.
  • Expect lowest-price awards for simple buys without competition. Make sure the catalogue entry carries the features the trust will filter on.
Source: GCA agreement page RM1557.15, read 20/10/2026.

Technology Services 4

Government Commercial Agency · RM6190 · open framework
LIVE
Dates
Start 12 December 2025, listed end 11 June 2028. GCA says it “will run for 8 years” and will reopen before the end of years 3 and 5.
Scope
Technology services, not software products: consultancy, service transition, end user services, infrastructure management, application and data management, and major transformation programmes. AI and automation were added as ancillary services.
Lots and suppliers
1 Technology and Digital Consultancy (212); 2 Transition and Service Integration and Management (SIAM) (149); 3 End User Services, minimum £3m (119); 3a under £3m (190); 4 Infrastructure Management, minimum £3m (114); 4a under £3m (188); 5 Application and Data Management, minimum £3m (126); 5a under £3m (214); 6 Major Technology Services Transformation Programmes (73).
Out of scope
IT hardware, telephony, managed print, contingent labour and management consultancy.
What to do
  • Use it for the services wrapped round your product: implementation, integration and data migration are often bought here by a partner, not by you.
  • Find your partners. The lot supplier lists are public on the GCA page. Check which integrators on lot 5 or 5a already work with your target trusts.
Source: GCA agreement page RM6190, supplier counts as shown on 20/10/2026.

Digital Primary Care Open Framework (the GP IT replacement)

NHS England · reference C454613 · open framework, competitive flexible procedure
PLANNED
Stage
Planned procurement notice published 6 October 2026. It states it is “not a ‘call to action’ notice”. Formal market engagement took place in May and June 2025.
Next dates (estimated)
Tender notice 9 November 2026. Award decision 5 August 2027. NHS England expects go-live in quarter 3 of 2027.
Framework term
20 September 2027 to 19 September 2030, with a possible extension to 19 September 2033 (six years). It is intended to reopen at least once in the first three years.
Value
£838,749,369 excluding VAT (estimated).
Lots
1 Foundation Electronic Patient Record (EPR) Solutions; 2 Clinical and Operational Support Solutions, 27 capabilities including clinical decision support, digital diagnostics, remote patient monitoring, medicines optimisation, risk stratification and video consultation; 3 Research and Development, which suppliers on lot 1 or 2 join automatically.
Who calls off
“Primarily by (but not limited to) Integrated Care Boards and general practices”, by direct award or competition.
Today’s route
Until then, NHS England’s National Commercial and Procurement Hub supports practices buying from the Digital Services for Integrated Care (DSIC) catalogue of frameworks, previously GP IT Futures.
What to do
  • Register now. The notice says suppliers must be registered on the government’s Central Digital Platform to be ready to bid.
  • Map your product to the lot 2 capability list in the notice’s attached “Description of Framework Capabilities”.
  • Sell to the ICB digital lead now. They will run the call-offs from late 2027.
Sources: Find a Tender notice 094419-2026; NHS England National Commercial and Procurement Hub; both read 20/10/2026.
Lists, not frameworks

Assurance lists buyers check first

Neither of these awards a contract. Each tells local buyers which products have met a national standard, and buyers then procure locally.

Digital social care records: Assured Solutions list

NHS England · web service marked beta
ASSURANCE LIST
What it is
An alphabetical list of digital social care record (DSCR) solutions assured by NHS England “in line with the DSCR Capability Assessment and Standards Assurance Process”. Every listed solution offers the core capabilities, and a list of those core capabilities can be downloaded.
Size
23 solutions listed on 20/10/2026.
Last updated
Not verified: the page carries no date.
What to do
  • Selling into care homes? Find out which assured records system the home uses before you pitch anything that needs to share data with it.
  • Selling a records system? Assurance is how you get on the list buyers are pointed to. The contact is on the list page.
Source: Assured solutions for digital social care records, read 20/10/2026.

Ambient voice technology self-certified supplier registry

NHS England, run by the National Commercial and Procurement Hub
REGISTRY
What it is
A registry of ambient voice technology (AVT) suppliers of AI-enabled ambient scribing products. NHS England states it “is not a procurement mechanism”: procurements “will be undertaken by the NHS bodies themselves”.
Entry requirements
Class 1 medical device status and a current NHS Digital Technology Assessment Criteria (DTAC) assessment, evidence of benefit in an NHS setting, a video showing the capabilities, a performance and monitoring response, and an indicative pricing matrix.
Dates
Preliminary market engagement notice first published 29 October 2025, with later versions on 3 February 2026 and 29 July 2026. First registry published 16 January 2026. It stays open for new submissions on an ongoing basis.
Who sees it
Shared with NHS buyers on enquiry to the National Commercial and Procurement Hub. Buyers such as GP practices, ICBs and acute trusts are expected to use it as part of their due diligence.
Suppliers listed
Not verified: the NHS England registry page could not be loaded for this check. Reading that page directly would settle the count.
What to do
  • Selling ambient scribing? Get listed. Without it, a buyer following NHS England guidance has a reason to stop.
  • Keep the evidence current. NHS England puts the duty to keep listing information up to date on the supplier.

Procurement Act 2023

The notices a rep should watch

Since 24 February 2025 every new procurement publishes a chain of notices on Find a Tender, linked by one procurement identifier. Read in order, they tell you how early you are.

UK1

Pipeline notice

Planned contracts over £2 million that will be tendered or directly awarded in the 18 months from 1 April. Published between 1 April and 26 May by bodies spending £100 million or more a year.

Do: read your trusts’ and ICBs’ pipelines every spring. It is the earliest public signal and the best time to start selling.
UK2

Preliminary market engagement

How the buyer will engage, or has engaged, with suppliers before the tender. Required before a tender if engagement happened.

Do: respond. This is where the specification and lots get shaped, as Technology Enabled Care Services 3 is doing now.
UK3

Planned procurement

Early warning of a competitive tender. Published 40 days to a year ahead, it can cut the tendering period to 10 days.

Do: treat it as a deadline alarm. Get registrations and evidence ready, as the GP IT replacement notice asks.
UK4

Tender notice

Invites tenders or requests to participate, including to set up a framework. Not used for awards under a framework.

Do: bid, or know which partner is bidding. Above threshold the normal minimum tendering period is 25 days.
UK6

Contract award notice

Published after the award decision and before signature, including for call-offs and direct awards. Starts a standstill period of at least 8 working days where one applies.

Do: log who won, at which trust, and for what. That is your competitor map and your renewal date.
UK7

Contract details notice

Published within 30 days of signature above threshold. For contracts over £5 million the redacted contract must be attached within 90 days.

Do: read the contract term and value, then diarise the end date 18 to 24 months ahead.
UK9

Contract performance

Reports performance against key performance indicators (KPIs), at least where KPIs were set, and breaches or failures to improve.

Do: watch the incumbent’s performance at your target trusts. A poor report is the opening for your conversation.
This quarter

Five things to do before Christmas

  1. Confirm your framework positions on G-Cloud 15, Technology Services 4 and, if relevant, the Digital Pathology lots. Put the reopening points in your diary.
  2. Prepare for the GP IT replacement tender, estimated for 9 November 2026: Central Digital Platform registration, and your product mapped to the lot 2 capabilities.
  3. Watch for the Technology Enabled Care Services 3 tender notice if you sell remote monitoring.
  4. Check your assurance listings: the ambient voice registry for scribing, and the Assured Solutions list for social care records.
  5. Set a weekly Find a Tender search on your target trusts and ICBs for contract award notices and contract details notices. That is how you see call-offs.
