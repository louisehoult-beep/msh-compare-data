The buying group

Nine roles you will meet on a digital deal

A digital product is rarely bought by one person. Executive digital leads sponsor it, safety and information roles can halt it, and procurement turns it into a contract. NHS England's letter of 29 July 2026 on ambient voice technology (AVT) was addressed to trust and ICB chief executives, chief information officers, chief nursing information officers and chief clinical information officers, and to NHS England regional directors of digital transformation. That addressee list is the clearest current statement of who NHS England expects to lead digital adoption.

Chief Information Officer (CIO)

Where the role is named

An addressee of NHS England's 29 July 2026 AVT letter, at both NHS trusts and ICBs.

What to bring them

Interoperability and scalability evidence. The letter tells organisations to buy AVT that can interface with the electronic patient record (EPR), natively or through full integration, and to weigh long-term interoperability, scalability and cyber security in procurement.

Source: NHS England, 29 July 2026

Chief Clinical Information Officer (CCIO)

Where the role is named

An addressee of the same letter, at trusts and ICBs.

What to bring them

Clinical workflow and accountability. The letter expects users to review and approve anything the technology generates before it is relied on for care, professional judgement to be kept, and clear lines of accountability for decisions informed by its outputs.

Source: NHS England, 29 July 2026

Chief Nursing Information Officer (CNIO)

Where the role is named

An addressee of the same letter, at trusts and ICBs.

What to bring them

Training and frontline adoption. The letter expects staff to be trained on the capabilities and limitations of the technology, and safe use to be built into existing clinical workflows rather than bolted on.

Source: NHS England, 29 July 2026

Chief Digital Officer

Where the role is named

Not verified as a national post. The title does not appear in the July 2026 letter's addressee list, and no national role definition was found. Where an organisation uses it, read that organisation's own board pages for the remit before you assume budget or sign-off sits there.

What to bring them

The same executive case you would take to the Chief Information Officer (CIO), framed against the organisation's published digital plan.

Settled by: the trust's or ICB's board membership page.

Senior Information Risk Owner (SIRO)

What the role is

Usually an executive director or member of the senior management board, with overall ownership of the organisation's information risk policy, and accountable and responsible for information risk across the organisation.

What to bring them

A clear account of the data your product holds, where it flows and how risks are controlled.

Source: National Data Guardian, Caldicott Guardian guidance, section 5.2

Caldicott Guardian

What the role is

A senior person responsible for protecting the confidentiality of people's health and care information and making sure it is used properly. All NHS organisations, and local authorities providing social services, must have one. In a large hospital trust it may be a senior clinician, and they must be free to raise concerns at senior management or board level.

What to bring them

How patient-identifiable information is used, shared and protected by your product.

Sources: UK Caldicott Guardian Council; National Data Guardian guidance

Clinical Safety Officer (CSO)

What the role is

Under DCB0160 (clinical risk management in the deployment and use of health information technology (IT) systems), top management must nominate a Clinical Safety Officer: a suitably qualified and experienced clinician, with current registration with a relevant professional body, knowledgeable in clinical risk management, who makes sure the process is followed.

What to bring them

Your clinical safety case report under DCB0129, the matching manufacturer standard. The deploying organisation needs it for its own risk analysis.

Source: NHS England Digital, DCB0160 specification v3.2, sections 2.2 to 2.5. NHS England has started a review of both standards; both remain published.

Procurement lead

What the role must check

DCB0160 requires the organisation, when procuring a health IT system, to ensure the manufacturer and the system comply with DCB0129. The July 2026 letter adds that procurement should assess clinical safety, information governance, cyber security, interoperability and regulatory compliance, and that contracts should give rights to monitor performance, audit compliance and manage changes in functionality, including at artificial intelligence (AI) model update milestones.

What to bring them

Compliance documents ready before they ask, and contract terms that already cover model updates.

Sources: DCB0160, section 2.5; NHS England, 29 July 2026

ICB digital lead

Where the role sits

ICB chief information officers, chief clinical information officers and chief nursing information officers were all addressed by the July 2026 letter. Each ICB can run its own Federated Data Platform (FDP) instance and is the data controller for it. NHS England's planning framework says ICBs should use the platform's population health management tools for strategic commissioning.

What to bring them

A system-wide case covering several trusts, and how your data fits population health work. Ask who in the NHS England region covers digital transformation too: those directors were also addressed.

Sources: NHS England, FDP infrastructure; Medium Term Planning Framework 2026/27 to 2028/29

Not verified: who holds digital budgets (trust capital, ICB allocation or a system pot). No national source sets this out, and it varies. Settle it account by account from the organisation's board papers and published digital strategy.

How decisions are made

Trust level and ICB level

The rules below are written into national standards and NHS England's own letters and planning guidance. They tell you which sign-offs a deal has to clear, whatever the local structure looks like.

At an NHS trust

  • Top management authorises go-live. Under DCB0160, top management must authorise deployment of a health IT system, accepting any residual clinical risk on the organisation's behalf, and must nominate a Clinical Safety Officer.
  • The board assures five areas. For AVT, NHS England asks boards and executive teams to assure themselves on oversight and accountability, local governance (clinical safety, information governance and digital assurance), staff training, risk management (privacy, consent, information quality and automation bias) and commercial and procurement arrangements.
  • Procurement checks the manufacturer. The trust must ensure the supplier complies with DCB0129 and can supply its clinical safety case report.
  • The plan sets the priorities. From April 2026, NHS England's planning framework asks providers to onboard to the FDP by 2028/29, deploy AVT at pace with regard to the national registry, reach full EPR coverage as soon as possible and meet the Digital Capabilities Framework minimum standards.

Sources: DCB0160; AVT letter; Medium Term Planning Framework

At an ICB

  • The ICB owns its own data platform. Each ICB can have its own FDP instance, acting for the Integrated Care System (ICS), and is its data controller. It can opt into nationally developed products.
  • Commissioning runs on that data. The planning framework says ICBs should use the FDP's population health management tools for strategic commissioning and adopt its System Coordination Centre and other performance tools.
  • Maturity evidence informs planning. NHS England describes the Digital Maturity Assessment (DMA) as an evidence base for system leaders' planning and strategic commissioning.
  • The region is in the loop. NHS England regional directors of digital transformation were named addressees of the AVT letter alongside ICB digital leads.

Sources: FDP infrastructure; Medium Term Planning Framework; DMA

The order a deal usually runs

Hub guidance, not a national rule. It is our reading of the sign-offs above, so check it against each account.

  1. An executive digital sponsor (CIO, CCIO or CNIO) links your product to a priority in the organisation's plan.
  2. The Clinical Safety Officer reviews your DCB0129 safety case and runs the local DCB0160 work.
  3. Information governance, the Caldicott Guardian and the Senior Information Risk Owner (SIRO) review data use and information risk.
  4. Procurement chooses a route and writes the contract terms, including terms for model updates.
  5. Top management authorises deployment and accepts the residual clinical risk.
What is driving spend now

Four national programmes behind the buying

Each of these gives you a reason to call, and a question that shows you know the account.

1. The NHS Federated Data Platform

Each trust and ICB can run its own instance of the platform and is the data controller for it. NHS England publishes uptake quarterly. The figures below were updated on 25 September 2026 and show uptake at the end of August 2026. The next update is due in December 2026.

Source: NHS England, FDP uptake and benefits, which links the trust-by-trust live list. NHS England's planning framework says acute trusts using the platform have seen an average increase of 114 elective operations a month per trust and a 35% reduction in delayed discharge days. That is NHS England's figure, quoted as published.

Rep angle: check whether your account is on the live list before the meeting. If it is not live, the framework still expects it to be by 2028/29, so the data work is coming either way.

2. The Digital Maturity Assessment

An annual self-assessment for all ICBs and NHS trusts in England against the seven dimensions of NHS England's What Good Looks Like (WGLL) framework: well led, smart foundations, safe practice, support people, empower citizens, improve care and healthy populations. It was built on a baseline of 50 questions. The report on the 2024 and 2025 results is published, with a 100% response rate. NHS England says the assessment is not a ranking or a judgement.

Sources: NHS England, Digital Maturity Assessment; DMA report, 2024 and 2025 results

Rep angle: ask which of the seven dimensions the organisation is working hardest to improve, and position your product against that dimension. Do not quote a trust's score back to it as a ranking.

3. The ambient voice technology letter, 29 July 2026

NHS England and the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) wrote to trusts, ICBs and regions on the day the MHRA published guidance on the regulatory status of AVT. Products used only to transcribe and summarise clinical conversations, draft correspondence or suggest clinical codes for clinician review are not regulated as medical devices under the current framework. Products that support diagnosis, treatment or automated clinical actions are regulated as medical devices.

  • Whatever their regulatory status, organisations should buy AVT that interfaces with the EPR, natively or through full integration.
  • National support starts in outpatient settings, with implementation support, commercial guidance and a benefits framework, then expands to other settings.
  • NHS England will set up an AVT Centre of Excellence with exemplar trusts. The self-certified supplier registry stays open.
  • The planning framework separately asks providers to deploy AVT at pace, with regard to the national registry.

Sources: NHS England, 29 July 2026 (reference PRN02607_i); Medium Term Planning Framework

4. The 10 Year Health Plan: digital commitments

Published on 3 July 2025, the plan commits to moving the NHS from analogue to digital. The commitments a digital buyer will cite:

  • A single patient record that gives patients control over one secure, authoritative account of their data.
  • By 2028, the NHS App will be a full front door to the entire NHS.
  • From April 2026, expanding the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) technology appraisal process, with mandated NHS funding, to some devices, diagnostics and digital products.
  • A "HealthStore" giving patients access to approved digital tools. The first will be those with the best NICE evidence, bought once nationally.
  • Single sign-on for staff and wider use of AI scribes, with a framework procurement that all NHS organisations can use in 2026 to 2027.
  • An "innovator passport" by 2026, so that once one NHS organisation has robustly assessed an innovation, others cannot insist on repeating the assessment. Whether it is live yet is not verified.
  • A national procurement for a new platform for community services, and wearables standard in preventative, chronic and post-acute care by 2035.

Source: Fit for the future: 10 Year Health Plan for England, GOV.UK

The adoption ecosystem

Who helps innovations into the NHS

These bodies do not usually sign the order, but they open doors, run evaluations and fund pilots. Buyers trust products that have been through them.

The 15 Health Innovation Networks

Established by NHS England in 2013 to spread innovation at pace and scale. Each works across its own geography, runs an Innovation Exchange for innovators and supports local ICSs with national programmes and real-world evaluation.

Source: NHS England, The Health Innovation Network

NHS Innovation Accelerator

A three-year fellowship that helps proven innovations scale across the NHS. It is funded by NHS England and delivered by UCLPartners in partnership with the 15 Health Innovation Networks.

Source: NHS Innovation Accelerator

SBRI Healthcare

The Small Business Research Initiative (SBRI) Healthcare is funded by NHS England in partnership with the Health Innovation Network. It funds early-stage feasibility and development work, and real-world NHS implementation studies for more mature products, through competitions themed on unmet NHS need. Round four of the NHS Cancer Programme Innovation Open Call opened on 14 October 2026.

Sources: SBRI Healthcare; SBRI Healthcare news

NICE HealthTech

NICE HealthTech guidance evaluates digital technologies, diagnostics and medical devices, including AI. Under the 10 Year Health Plan, NICE runs the National HealthTech Access Programme with the Department of Health and Social Care, NHS England, the MHRA and the Office for Life Sciences. Technologies it recommends will get funded NHS use. Its first focus is two technologies for early diagnosis of oesophageal, prostate and breast cancer.

Source: NICE, About HealthTech guidance

Qualify the buyer

Ten questions to qualify a digital buyer

Hub guidance. Each question is tied to a rule or programme above, so it shows you understand how the NHS buys digital products.

  1. Which priority in your 2026/27 plan does this serve? Tie it to the planning framework's digital expectations, such as the FDP, AVT, EPR coverage or NHS App adoption.
  2. Who is the executive sponsor, and which board or committee authorises deployment? Under DCB0160, top management accepts the residual clinical risk.
  3. Who is your Clinical Safety Officer, and what do they need from our DCB0129 safety case?
  4. Have your information governance team, Caldicott Guardian and SIRO seen how our data flows?
  5. Which EPR do you run, and do you need native or full integration? For AVT, NHS England expects EPR integration whatever the product's regulatory status.
  6. Is your organisation live on the Federated Data Platform, and does our product need to work with your instance?
  7. Which What Good Looks Like dimension are you working hardest to improve?
  8. Is the budget held by the trust, the ICB or across the system, and who signs at each level?
  9. How will you buy: an existing framework, your own tender or a regional collaboration? Then ask what contract rights you will want at AI model updates.
  10. Has another NHS organisation or a Health Innovation Network already evaluated this? If so, ask whether you can rely on that assessment rather than repeat it.

Before you go in, read the users' view. Medical Consulting Group's Resident Doctors and NHS IT Systems (June 2026) interviewed ten Foundation Year doctors: three or more disconnected systems per patient encounter, each with its own login, and slow or scarce hardware holding back every new tool. If your product adds a login, expect the ward to push back. Free with registration, listed in Downloads.
