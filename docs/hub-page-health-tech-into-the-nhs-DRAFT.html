Health tech into the NHS

A digital product meets a run of gates before an NHS buyer can say yes: regulation, clinical safety, data protection, evidence and a buying route. Each gate below is linked to the body that owns it, and each fact was read at that body's own page.

Every source read 20 October 2026

The gates, in order

What a product needs before an NHS buyer can say yes

Gate 1 · Regulation

Registration with the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA)

The MHRA states that all medical devices, including in vitro diagnostic devices, must be registered with the MHRA before they can be placed on the market in Great Britain. A manufacturer based outside the UK must appoint a single UK Responsible Person, who then registers its devices.

Source: MHRA, Register medical devices to place on the market, last updated 16 October 2026.

Gate 1 · Regulation

CE marking: accepted in Great Britain for now

The MHRA's guidance says Conformité Européenne (CE) marked devices may be placed on the Great Britain market until either 30 June 2028 or 30 June 2030, depending on the type of device and the European Union legislation it complies with. The same page says the government will consult on indefinite recognition of CE marked medical devices.

Source: MHRA, Regulating medical devices in the UK, last updated 20 February 2026.

Gate 2 · Clinical safety

DCB0129 for manufacturers, DCB0160 for the NHS body deploying

NHS England's clinical risk management standards: DCB0129, Clinical Risk Management: its Application in the Manufacture of Health IT Systems, and DCB0160, which covers the deploying health organisation. The page states that Compliance with DCB0129 and DCB0160 is mandatory under the Health and Social care Act 2012.

Source: NHS England Digital, Clinical risk management standards, last edited 11 October 2024.

Gate 3 · Assurance

Digital Technology Assessment Criteria (DTAC)

DTAC covers clinical safety, data protection, technical security, interoperability, and usability and accessibility. NHS England's refreshed form has a 25% reduction in questions, and the page states that The previous version of the DTAC form should not be used from 6 April 2026 onwards.

Source: NHS England Digital, Digital Technology Assessment Criteria, last edited 21 September 2026.

Gate 4 · Data protection

Data Security and Protection Toolkit (DSPT)

An online self-assessment against the National Data Guardian's 10 data security standards. The toolkit's own page states that All organisations that have access to NHS patient data and systems must use this toolkit to provide assurance on data security.

Source: Data Security and Protection Toolkit home page.

Gate 5 · Evidence

National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) HealthTech guidance

NICE's HealthTech guidance evaluates digital technologies, diagnostics and medical devices, including AI. Its National HealthTech Access Programme, developed with the Department of Health and Social Care, NHS England, the MHRA and the Office for Life Sciences, will evaluate a small number of technologies; those recommended for NHS use will receive national NHS funding, similar to medicines.

Source: NICE, About HealthTech guidance.

What is changing

The rules for AI-enabled devices are being rewritten

All 44 recommendations accepted

On 6 October 2026 the government confirmed it will accept all 44 recommendations of the National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare. The MHRA is committed to issue draft guidance by December 2026 on a new approach to managing changes to AI-enabled medical devices, and will begin next year by consulting on how to qualify and classify AI-enabled devices.

Source: Government backs recommendations of NHS doctors-led AI Commission, GOV.UK, 6 October 2026.

AI Airlock, phase 3

The MHRA's regulatory sandbox for AI-enabled medical devices opened its third phase on 6 October 2026, with applications through the form on its page. Its second phase completed in May 2026 with 7 innovators across 3 regulatory challenges.

Source: MHRA, AI Airlock: a regulatory sandbox for AI-enabled medical devices, last updated 6 October 2026.

Buying routes and adoption

Where digital is bought, and who is already live

G-Cloud 15 (RM1557.15)

The Government Commercial Agency (GCA) agreement for cloud hosting, software and support, open to health among other sectors. Its page lists a start date of 6 August 2026 and an end date of 5 February 2028, and says G-Cloud 15 will run for 4 years from 6 August 2026, reopening after 18 months and again after 36 months. Crown Commercial Service became the GCA on 1 April 2026.

Source: GCA, G-Cloud 15.

NHS Federated Data Platform (FDP)

NHS England reports 142 NHS trusts live on the platform and 35 integrated care boards (ICBs) with a tenant, as at the end of August 2026, with 170 trusts signed up. It updated these figures on 25 September 2026; the next update is due in December 2026.

Source: NHS England, NHS Federated Data Platform uptake and benefits.
