Who it is for

Nurses who work in industry, and the companies that employ them

Nurses in industry sit between the clinic and the company. The work is different from ward or community nursing, and the profession has not always had a place of its own for it. This association is intended to be that place.

Clinical advisors and specialists

Nurses who support products and services in clinical settings and advise on how they are used.

Product and training roles

Product managers, product specialists and trainers who bring clinical practice into how products are built, explained and taught.

Employers

Companies that employ nurses can register too, and tell us how many nurses they employ, so the association reflects the whole sector.

The roles

What nurses do in industry

Job titles vary from company to company, and the same job can carry different names. These are the kinds of role this association is for.

Clinical advisor or clinical specialist

Supports customers and clinical teams in using a product safely and well, often at the bedside, in clinic or in training sessions.

Product specialist

Demonstrates a product to clinicians and helps them judge how it fits their patients and their pathway.

Product manager

Brings clinical practice into how a product is specified, improved and positioned.

Trainer or educator

Designs and delivers training so that staff can use products and services confidently.

Clinical safety, quality and regulatory

Helps a company meet the safety and quality standards that apply to what it sells into the NHS.

Sales and account roles

Nurses who use their clinical background to work with NHS and care customers.

The companies

Who employs nurses in industry

Nurses work for companies of every size that supply the NHS and the wider care sector, from small specialist suppliers to large multinationals. Typical sectors include medical devices, wound care, diagnostics, digital health, and pharmaceutical and clinical services. If your employer is not on that list, you are still welcome.

Companies can register as employers and tell us how many nurses they employ. That gives the association a true picture of the sector, and it is how we will learn where nurses in industry are, and what they need.

Your questions

The questions we want to ask you

We would rather ask than assume. Join, and we will ask these when we write to you. Your answers will help decide what the association does first.

Do you find it hard to get your revalidation hours?

The Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC) asks nurses for 450 practice hours and 35 hours of continuing professional development (CPD), at least 20 of them participatory, in each three-year cycle. The NMC's page says hours count when you rely on your skills, knowledge and experience as a registered nurse, but it does not say whether industry roles count, so many nurses are left to work it out for themselves.

Questions people ask

Before you join

Is this for occupational health nurses?

No. Occupational health nurses look after the health of a workforce, and they have their own professional groups. This association is for nurses employed by companies that supply health and care, such as medical device, pharmaceutical, diagnostics and digital health companies.

Who can join?

Nurses working in industry, and companies that employ them. The form asks for your professional registration, including midwives and nursing associates. If yours is not listed, choose Other.

Does it cost anything?

No. Joining is free, and so is the Medical Sales Hub access that comes with it.

How do I get my Medical Sales Hub access?

After you join, we email you a link. Opening it confirms your email address and takes you to a page where you set up your Hub account.

Does joining count towards my revalidation?

Joining does not give you continuing professional development (CPD) hours. What counts for revalidation is set by the NMC. Their pages are in the useful links above.

Who runs the association?

Elevate and Thrive Ltd (company no. 17154474) runs the association while it is being set up, and members will help shape what it becomes. It is not run or endorsed by the NMC, the RCN, ABHI or any employer.

What happens to my details?

Your answers are used to run the association and keep you informed. You can unsubscribe from our emails at any time. The privacy policy explains how we handle your data.
